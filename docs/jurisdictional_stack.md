# The Jurisdictional Stack: Four Layers of Legal Reality

Every legal interaction occurs within a jurisdiction. Courts, orders, statutes, and rights all derive their authority from a specific jurisdictional layer. The critical skill is identifying WHICH layer is active — because different layers grant different rights, impose different obligations, and respond to different challenges.

Courts routinely move between layers without disclosure. A family court may begin in statutory jurisdiction (750 ILCS 5/503) and shift to commercial jurisdiction (treating the parties as corporate entities settling debts) without ever announcing the change. Recognizing this shift is the first step toward challenging it.

---

## Layer 4: NATURAL LAW / DIVINE LAW

**Authority source:** Nature, creation, inherent existence. Not granted by any state, court, or legislature.

**Core principle:** Rights exist by virtue of being alive. They precede government. They cannot be granted because they were never absent. They cannot be revoked because no human institution created them.

**Rights at this layer:**
- The parent-offspring bond. A mother's right to her child is not granted by a court — it exists by nature. A court order that severs this bond must justify its authority to override natural law.
- Bodily autonomy. No entity has jurisdiction over your body without your consent.
- Freedom of movement. The right to travel on the land without license or permission.
- Self-determination. The right to direct your own life, labor, and property.

**Vocabulary:** offspring (not "child" — which has legal definitions), living soul, flesh and blood, man, woman, DNA, natural person, creator, endowed, unalienable.

**Key distinction:** Natural rights are UNALIENABLE — they cannot be transferred, sold, or surrendered. They can only be violated. A court that claims to "grant" custody is operating below this layer, because natural law recognizes no authority to grant what already exists.

**How to recognize it:** If a document references "God-given rights," "natural rights," "the laws of nature," or "inherent rights" — it is invoking Layer 4.

**How courts avoid it:** Courts almost never acknowledge natural law because it limits their jurisdiction. If a parent asserts natural law rights, the court must either acknowledge a higher authority than itself or ignore the claim entirely. Both responses reveal the court's jurisdictional limits.

**Example:** "Do you claim authority to sever the bond between a mother and her offspring? Under what law of nature does that authority exist?"

## Layer 3: COMMON LAW

**Authority source:** Custom, precedent, the consent of the governed. Built over centuries of practice and agreement.

**Core principle:** Rights exist because they have been recognized and defended through history. The common law is the law of the people — not the law of the legislature or the crown.

**Rights at this layer:**
- Trial by jury of peers. Not a panel of lawyers — actual peers from the community.
- Habeas corpus. The right to challenge detention, to demand that the state justify holding you.
- Due process. Proper notice, opportunity to be heard, impartial tribunal.
- Property rights. Real property (land) held in allodial title — owned outright, not held from a superior.
- Right to contract. Voluntary agreements between consenting parties.

**Vocabulary:** man, woman, the people, land, property, oath, affidavit, common law wife/husband, freehold, allodial title, jury, peers, custom, precedent, writ.

**Key distinction:** Common law requires a HARMED PARTY. No victim, no crime. This is fundamentally different from statutory law, where the state can prosecute without any individual being harmed.

**How to recognize it:** References to Magna Carta, the Bill of Rights (as original common law protections), trial by jury, habeas corpus, or "the common law of the land."

**How courts suppress it:** Modern courts operate primarily in statutory or equity jurisdiction. When a party invokes common law rights (e.g., demanding a jury trial in a family court), the court may claim those rights "don't apply" in this context — revealing that the court is NOT operating under common law.

**Example:** "Is this court operating under the common law? If so, where is the injured party? If not, under what jurisdiction does it claim authority?"

## Layer 2: STATUTORY LAW / EQUITY

**Authority source:** Legislation, codes, court rules. Created by legislatures and enforced by courts.

**Core principle:** Rights and obligations are defined by written statutes. The legislature creates the rules; the courts interpret and enforce them. Equity jurisdiction allows courts to issue orders based on "fairness" and "discretion" rather than strict legal rights.

**This is where most legal proceedings operate by default.** Divorce cases, custody disputes, protective orders — all are governed by statute (e.g., 750 ILCS 5/503 in Illinois) and adjudicated in equity.

**Rights at this layer:**
- Whatever the statute grants. Statutory rights are PRIVILEGES — they can be modified or revoked by the legislature.
- Equitable remedies: injunctions, orders of protection, custody arrangements.
- Procedural rights as defined by court rules (Supreme Court Rules, local rules).

**Vocabulary:** petitioner, respondent, order, judgment, statute, code, section, subsection, motion, hearing, ruling, finding, best interest of the child, equitable distribution.

**Key distinction:** Equity has NO JURY. A judge decides based on discretion. The "best interest of the child" standard is an equity doctrine — it gives the judge nearly unlimited power to decide outcomes based on subjective judgment. This is why family courts feel arbitrary: they ARE arbitrary, by design.

**How to recognize it:** Citations to specific statutes (750 ILCS, 28 U.S.C.), references to "the court's discretion," "equitable distribution," "best interest," or "this court finds."

**Critical insight:** By filing a petition in a statutory court, you CONSENT to its jurisdiction. The act of filing is the act of submission. This is why the question "Do I consent to this jurisdiction?" must be asked BEFORE engaging with the system — not after.

**Example:** "Under which specific statute does this court claim jurisdiction over the natural bond between parent and offspring?"

## Layer 1: COMMERCIAL / ADMIRALTY LAW

**Authority source:** The law of commerce, contracts between corporate entities, the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC), admiralty/maritime law.

**Core principle:** Everything is a commercial transaction. Parties are corporate entities. Disputes are about debts, obligations, and the settlement of accounts. The court is a venue for commercial arbitration.

**This layer often operates WITHOUT DISCLOSURE.** A family court may be functioning as a commercial court — treating the parties as corporate fictions (JOHN DOE vs. JANE DOE), treating children as assets to be divided, treating custody as a commercial arrangement — without ever telling the parties.

**Rights at this layer:**
- Contract rights as defined by the UCC.
- Secured creditor/debtor relationships.
- Commercial paper, negotiable instruments.
- Corporate rights and obligations.

**Vocabulary:** PERSON (all caps), DEFENDANT, PLAINTIFF, CREDITOR, DEBTOR, CORPORATION, TRUST, ESTATE, ASSET, LIABILITY, SETTLEMENT, ACCOUNT. Note the ALL CAPS names — this is a marker of the corporate fiction.

**Key distinction:** In commercial law, your name in ALL CAPITALS (JOEL THORARINSON) is not you — it is a corporate entity, a legal fiction created by the state (birth certificate, social security number). The court has jurisdiction over the FICTION, not over the living man or woman. The trick is that most people do not know the difference, and by responding to the ALL CAPS name, they accept jurisdiction over their person.

**How to recognize it:**
- Names in ALL CAPITALS on court documents
- Language of debt and settlement ("division of marital assets" = settling accounts between corporate entities)
- References to the UCC, commercial code, or banking regulations
- Treatment of children as "assets" to be "allocated"
- Focus on financial obligations rather than rights

**How courts conceal it:** The court never announces "we are now operating in commercial jurisdiction." It simply uses commercial language and procedures while maintaining the appearance of a statutory proceeding. The shift happens in vocabulary, not in formal declarations.

**Example:** "Is the name on that order the name of a living man, or is it the name of a corporate fiction? Under what authority does this court claim jurisdiction over the living man behind that fiction?"

## How Courts Move Between Layers

Courts shift jurisdiction constantly, and the shift is almost always DOWNWARD — from higher rights to lower ones.

**The typical sequence in a family court case:**
1. Natural law says the parent-offspring bond is sacred and cannot be severed.
2. Common law says you have a right to a jury trial and due process.
3. The court operates in EQUITY (Layer 2), where the judge has "discretion" and there is no jury.
4. The court's actual mechanism is COMMERCIAL (Layer 1), treating the family as a set of assets and debts to be divided.

**Each downward shift strips rights.** Moving from natural law to equity eliminates the parent's inherent rights and replaces them with "the best interest of the child" — a standard that the judge alone controls. Moving from equity to commercial eliminates even the statutory protections and treats the case as a business transaction.

**The strategic response:** Ask questions that force the court to identify its jurisdictional layer. If it claims equity jurisdiction, ask where the consent was given. If it claims statutory jurisdiction, ask which statute overrides natural law. If it operates commercially, ask why the parties are being treated as corporate fictions.

The court cannot answer these questions without either:
1. Acknowledging a higher jurisdiction it is violating, or
2. Revealing that it has no authority beyond what the parties consented to.

Either answer shifts the strategic position.
