# Court Jurisdiction Analysis

How to identify what kind of court you are actually in — not what it calls itself, but how it operates.

A court's name means nothing. "Family Court," "Circuit Court," "Superior Court" — these are labels. What matters is the JURISDICTION the court exercises: the source of its authority, the rules it follows, and the rights it recognizes. A "Family Court" may operate as a banking court settling debts between corporate fictions. A "Circuit Court" may operate in equity with no jury and no due process protections.

**The fundamental question: "Under what authority does this court operate?"**

If the court cannot or will not answer, that is itself an answer.

---

## Court Type Markers

### Banking / Commercial Court (Layer 1)

A court operating in commercial/admiralty jurisdiction treats the case as a financial transaction between corporate entities.

**Language markers:**
- Names in ALL CAPITALS (JOHN DOE, JANE DOE)
- "Division of marital assets" — treating family property as corporate assets
- "Child support obligation" — framing the parent-child relationship as a debt
- "Settlement" — commercial term for resolving a financial dispute
- "Order to pay" — commercial directive
- References to "accounts," "arrears," "garnishment"
- "Maintenance" or "alimony" — commercial terms for ongoing financial obligations

**Procedural markers:**
- No jury — commercial disputes are decided by an arbitrator (the judge)
- Summary proceedings — quick resolution, like commercial arbitration
- Focus on financial disclosure, asset valuation, income calculation
- Guardian ad litem treated as an auditor assessing asset allocation
- "Best interest of the child" used as a formula for asset distribution

**Authority markers:**
- The court derives authority from the parties' participation (filing = consent)
- Court acts as a commercial venue, not a rights-based tribunal
- Judge acts as arbitrator between corporate entities, not as a protector of rights
- Enforcement through contempt (commercial penalty) rather than criminal process

**Key question:** "Is this court operating as a commercial venue settling debts between corporate fictions, or as a court of law adjudicating rights between living men and women?"

### Congressional / Statutory Court (Layer 2)

A court operating in statutory jurisdiction applies legislation — codes, acts, and rules created by the legislature.

**Language markers:**
- Citations to specific statutes: "750 ILCS 5/503," "28 U.S.C. 1332"
- "Pursuant to Section..." — operating under legislative authority
- "The statute provides..." — the legislature is the source of law
- "As defined in [code]..." — definitions come from statutes, not nature or custom
- "The court finds..." — applying statutory standards
- References to "public policy" — the legislature's declared priorities

**Procedural markers:**
- Follows codified rules of procedure (Illinois Supreme Court Rules, Federal Rules)
- Motions, briefs, hearings — all defined by statute
- Strict timelines set by statute (30 days to respond, 60 days to trial)
- Discovery procedures as defined by rules
- Appeals based on statutory interpretation

**Authority markers:**
- Authority flows from the legislature through the statute
- The judge interprets and applies the statute — does not create law
- Jurisdiction is defined by the statute itself (subject matter + personal)
- Can be challenged by questioning the statute's applicability or constitutionality

**Key question:** "Which specific statute grants this court jurisdiction over the natural relationship between parent and offspring? Does that statute override the rights that preexist it?"

### Equity Court (Layer 2, subset)

Equity jurisdiction is a subset of statutory jurisdiction that gives the judge extraordinary discretion. Family courts operate primarily in equity.

**Language markers:**
- "Best interest of the child" — the signature phrase of equity jurisdiction
- "The court, in its discretion..." — unlimited judicial power
- "Equitable distribution" — dividing property by "fairness," not by right
- "This court finds it equitable to..." — subjective standard
- "Balancing the factors..." — no fixed rule, judge weighs as they choose
- "Modified circumstances" — equity allows constant re-litigation

**Procedural markers:**
- NO JURY. This is the critical marker. If there is no jury, the court is operating in equity, not at common law.
- Judge is both finder of fact AND arbiter of law
- Standards are subjective ("best interest," "equity," "fairness")
- Orders can be modified at any time based on "changed circumstances"
- Broad contempt power — the judge can jail for disobeying equitable orders

**Authority markers:**
- Authority comes from the concept of "equity" — fairness as determined by the judge
- Historical origin: the chancellor of England, acting on behalf of the king, overriding common law when it produced "unjust" results
- The judge IS the jurisdiction — their discretion is the law
- Almost impossible to appeal because the standard is "abuse of discretion" (very high bar)

**Critical insight:** Equity jurisdiction means YOU HAVE NO FIXED RIGHTS. Your rights are whatever the judge says they are on that day. The "best interest" standard is not a rule — it is the absence of rules. This is why family court feels lawless: in equity, it is.

**Key question:** "If this court operates in equity, by what consent did I surrender my common law right to a trial by jury? Was that consent informed and voluntary?"

### Common Law Court (Layer 3)

A court operating under common law applies precedent, custom, and the rights recognized through centuries of practice.

**Language markers:**
- References to precedent: "In accordance with [prior case]..."
- "The common law provides..." — custom is the source of law
- "Rights of the accused" — rights-focused language
- "Trial by jury" — the hallmark of common law
- "Habeas corpus" — the right to challenge detention
- "Due process of law" — not statutory procedure, but fundamental fairness

**Procedural markers:**
- Jury trial as of right
- Adversarial process — two parties present evidence, jury decides
- Strict rules of evidence (because the jury must be protected from prejudice)
- Presumption of innocence (in criminal matters)
- Requirement of a harmed party — no victim, no case

**Authority markers:**
- Authority derives from the people, through custom and precedent
- The judge is a referee, not the decision-maker — the jury decides facts
- Rights preexist the court and cannot be created or destroyed by it
- Limited jurisdiction — the court can only act where it has been given authority by the people

**How to get here:** Demand a jury trial. Assert common law rights. Refuse equity jurisdiction. File a demand for common law proceeding. The court will resist because common law limits its power. That resistance is evidence that the court is NOT operating under common law — and that fact must be put on the record.

**Key question:** "Is this court prepared to empanel a jury of my peers to hear this matter under the common law? If not, under what jurisdiction does it claim to operate?"

## Analysis Framework

When examining any court document (order, ruling, filing, transcript), apply this checklist:

### 1. Name Format
- [ ] Are names in ALL CAPITALS? → Commercial jurisdiction likely
- [ ] Are names in standard format? → Statutory or common law
- [ ] Are parties identified by role (Petitioner/Respondent)? → Statutory
- [ ] Are parties identified as man/woman? → Common law or natural law

### 2. Authority Cited
- [ ] Specific statutes cited? → Statutory jurisdiction
- [ ] "Court's discretion" invoked? → Equity
- [ ] Commercial/UCC language? → Commercial jurisdiction
- [ ] Precedent/custom cited? → Common law
- [ ] No authority cited at all? → The court is asserting naked power — challenge immediately

### 3. Rights Framework
- [ ] "Best interest of the child"? → Equity (no fixed rights)
- [ ] Financial focus (assets, debts, support)? → Commercial
- [ ] Rights explicitly protected? → Common law
- [ ] Natural rights acknowledged? → Layer 4 (rare in modern courts)

### 4. Procedural Markers
- [ ] Jury available? → Common law possible
- [ ] No jury, judge decides all? → Equity or commercial
- [ ] Summary proceedings? → Commercial
- [ ] Full adversarial process? → Common law

### 5. Enforcement Mechanism
- [ ] Contempt of court? → Equity (judge's personal power)
- [ ] Garnishment/liens? → Commercial
- [ ] Criminal prosecution? → Statutory
- [ ] Community enforcement? → Common law

## The Question Protocol

For any court proceeding, these questions establish jurisdiction on the record:

1. "Under what specific authority does this court claim jurisdiction over this matter?"
2. "Is this court operating under common law, equity, statutory, or commercial jurisdiction?"
3. "If this court operates in equity, when did I consent to waive my common law rights?"
4. "Does this court recognize the natural law rights of a parent to their offspring?"
5. "Is the name on that document the name of a living man/woman, or the name of a corporate fiction?"
6. "Where is the contract that establishes this court's jurisdiction over me?"

These questions cannot be answered without revealing the court's jurisdictional basis — which is exactly the point.
