# Legal Title vs. Equitable Title

The distinction between legal title and equitable title is one of the most powerful — and most concealed — concepts in law. Understanding it reveals how courts claim authority they may not possess and how the system separates people from their natural rights.

---

## The Two Titles

### Legal Title

**Definition:** Record ownership. The name on the paper. The entity recognized by the state as the "owner" of something.

Legal title is a REGISTRATION. When the state records your name as the owner of a house, a car, or — critically — as the custodial parent of a child, it is granting legal title. The key word is "granting" — which means it was the state's to give.

Legal title carries:
- The right to be listed as owner in public records
- The obligation to follow the rules attached to that title (taxes, regulations, court orders)
- Subjection to the jurisdiction that granted the title
- The appearance of ownership without necessarily the substance of it

**Example — Property:** You "own" your house, but try not paying property taxes. The state will take it. This reveals that your "ownership" is actually legal title — a permission from the state to use the property, revocable if you break the state's rules. You have the paper; the state has the power.

**Example — Custody:** A court "grants" custody to a parent. This is legal title — the state's recognition that you may have possession of the child. It implies that without the court's grant, you would NOT have this right. It makes the state the source of the parent-child relationship.

---

### Equitable Title

**Definition:** Beneficial interest. The right to use, enjoy, and benefit from something. The substance behind the paper.

Equitable title is the ACTUAL RELATIONSHIP. When a mother nurses her child, when a father teaches his son, when a family lives together — that is equitable title. It exists regardless of what any court says. No document creates it; no order destroys it.

Equitable title carries:
- The actual use and enjoyment of the thing
- The natural relationship (parent-offspring bond)
- No dependence on state recognition
- The substance of ownership, even without the paper

**Example — Property:** A person builds a house with their own hands on land they have occupied for decades. They have equitable title — the beneficial use and enjoyment — even if legal title is held by another entity on paper.

**Example — Custody:** A mother who has raised her child since birth has equitable title to the parent-child relationship. A court order that "grants custody" to someone else transfers LEGAL title (the paper) but cannot transfer the natural bond (equitable title). The mother's equitable interest survives the court's order — the court just pretends it doesn't.

---

## How Courts Conflate the Two

The court system's power depends on confusing legal title with equitable title — on making people believe that the paper IS the reality.

### The Conflation in Custody

When a court says "I grant custody to the father," it is:
1. Asserting that it has authority over the parent-child relationship (jurisdiction claim)
2. Transferring LEGAL title (the state's recognition) from one parent to another
3. IMPLYING that equitable title (the natural bond) follows legal title
4. Acting as if the child is property that can be allocated by judicial order

But equitable title — the natural bond between parent and offspring — is not the court's to transfer. A court can change the name on the paper. It cannot change the blood in the veins.

### The Conflation in Property

When a court "divides marital assets," it is:
1. Treating all property as if legal title = total ownership
2. Ignoring equitable interests that may exist independently
3. Operating as a commercial court settling accounts between corporate entities
4. Assuming jurisdiction over the substance of ownership, not just the record

### Why Courts Do This

If courts acknowledged the distinction between legal and equitable title in custody cases, they would have to admit:
- They can only control the PAPER, not the REALITY
- Natural rights (equitable title) exist above their jurisdiction
- "Granting custody" is a legal fiction — the real relationship exists independent of the order
- Their authority over families is limited to those who consent to it

## The Corporate Fiction Connection

This is where the layers converge.

### Your Birth Certificate Creates a Legal Title

When a child is born, the state issues a birth certificate. This document creates a LEGAL ENTITY — a corporate fiction — with a name in ALL CAPITALS (or formatted as a state record). The state holds legal title to this entity. The living man or woman behind the entity holds equitable title.

**Legal title (the state holds):** JOEL THORARINSON — a corporate fiction, a registered entity, subject to statutes, courts, and commercial law.

**Equitable title (the living man holds):** Joel, son of Thor — a living man, flesh and blood, subject only to natural law and his own conscience.

When a court issues an order directed at "JOEL THORARINSON," it is exercising jurisdiction over the LEGAL ENTITY — the corporate fiction. It has legal title over that fiction. But it does NOT have equitable title over the living man — unless the living man consents by responding as if he IS the fiction.

### How This Applies to Custody

1. The court has jurisdiction over the LEGAL ENTITY (the PERSON named in the filing)
2. The court issues an order transferring legal title ("custody") from one PERSON to another
3. The court claims this transfer applies to the LIVING CHILD — conflating the legal entity with the living being
4. The parent who accepts this framework has consented to the fiction

### The Challenge

"Are you issuing this order against the living man/woman, or against the corporate entity? If against the corporate entity, by what authority do you extend that order to the living being? If against the living man/woman, under what jurisdiction — natural law, common law, or commercial — do you claim that authority?"

## Strategic Applications

### Separating Legal from Equitable in Court

1. **Never accept that legal title = equitable title.** When the court says "custody is granted to X," respond: "Legal title may be transferred by this court, but the equitable interest — the natural bond between parent and offspring — is not within this court's jurisdiction."

2. **Demand disclosure of which title is being affected.** "Is this order affecting legal title only, or is the court claiming to affect the equitable relationship between a living mother and her living offspring?"

3. **Preserve equitable title on the record.** "For the record, I assert my equitable interest in the parent-child relationship, which exists by nature and is not subject to the jurisdiction of this court."

### Questions That Expose the Distinction

- "Does this court acknowledge the difference between legal title and equitable title?"
- "By what authority does this court claim to transfer equitable interest in a parent-child bond?"
- "Is the 'custody' being discussed legal custody (a paper designation) or is the court claiming authority over the natural relationship itself?"
- "If the court is only transferring legal title, does the equitable interest of the natural parent survive?"
- "Has this court informed the parties that their equitable rights may differ from their legal title?"

---

## The Bottom Line

Legal title is the map. Equitable title is the territory. Courts control the map. They want you to believe the map IS the territory. The moment you understand that the paper is not the reality — that a court order transferring "custody" does not transfer the parent-child bond — you begin to see the limits of the court's actual authority.

The question is never "What does the court order say?" The question is: "What authority does the court have over the living reality behind the paper?"
