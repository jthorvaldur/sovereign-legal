# Black's Law Translations Across Jurisdictions

Words are jurisdictional weapons. The same word carries different meanings in different jurisdictions, and using the wrong word in the wrong context accepts the wrong jurisdiction. A mother who says "I want custody of my child" has already surrendered two layers of rights:

1. "Custody" is a statutory/commercial term — it implies the state has authority to grant or deny it.
2. "Child" in Black's Law has specific legal definitions that may not align with the natural relationship.

The same mother who says "I am the mother of my offspring and no court has authority to sever that bond" is operating in natural law — a jurisdiction where no court has inherent power.

**This is not wordplay. This is strategy.**

## Translation Table

| Term | Common English | Black's Law (Statutory) | Natural Rights (Layer 4/3) | Commercial/Admiralty (Layer 1) |
|------|---------------|------------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Custody** | Having care of someone | Legal right to control and maintain a person; the care, control, and maintenance of a child awarded by a court | Stewardship of offspring — a natural duty, not a state-granted right | Possession of an asset; physical control of property or a person in the context of a commercial arrangement |
| **Child** | Young human being | A person under the age of majority; a ward of the state when under court jurisdiction; includes adopted children | Offspring — a living being connected by blood and nature to the parent; not a legal category but a natural fact | A dependent; a liability or asset on a balance sheet; a trust beneficiary |
| **Person** | A human being | A legal entity with rights and obligations; includes natural persons AND corporations; "person" in a statute often means "corporate entity" | Man or woman — a living soul, flesh and blood; not a legal construct | PERSON — the corporate fiction, the ALL CAPS name on government documents; the entity the state creates and has jurisdiction over |
| **Property** | Things you own | Real or personal property as defined by statute; subject to taxation, regulation, and state claims; includes "marital property" subject to division | That which a man or woman holds by right of labor and nature; allodial property held without obligation to any superior | Commercial asset; subject to liens, encumbrances, UCC filings; "marital property" = corporate assets to be divided between entities |
| **Title** | Ownership | The legal right to ownership as recognized by the state; legal title (record ownership) vs equitable title (beneficial interest) | Natural dominion — authority over one's own body, labor, and possessions by right of existence | Certificate of ownership of a commercial asset; subject to liens and superior claims |
| **Order** | A command | A directive of a court, enforceable by contempt; the court's exercise of jurisdiction over the parties | A request — no man has authority to command another absent consent or a harmed party | A commercial directive between entities within an agreed framework; enforceable only within the commercial relationship |
| **Judgment** | A decision | The final determination of a court of competent jurisdiction; creates obligations enforceable by law | An opinion — carries no weight absent jurisdiction over the living man or woman | Settlement of accounts; final determination of debts and obligations between corporate entities |
| **Court** | Place where justice happens | A tribunal established by law for the administration of justice; derives authority from statute or constitution | An assembly — a place where men and women come together to resolve disputes by reason and evidence | A commercial venue for the settlement of disputes between corporate entities; operates under admiralty or UCC jurisdiction |
| **Jurisdiction** | Authority over a matter | The power of a court to hear and decide a case; requires subject-matter jurisdiction AND personal jurisdiction | Authority that exists only by consent — no man has inherent jurisdiction over another | Commercial jurisdiction created by contract — filing a case = signing a contract = accepting jurisdiction |
| **Consent** | Agreement | Voluntary agreement by a person of sufficient capacity; can be express or implied; silence may constitute consent | Informed, voluntary, explicit agreement — cannot be coerced, tricked, or presumed | Acceptance of terms; entering the court system = consenting to commercial jurisdiction; using ALL CAPS name = consenting to the fiction |
| **Contract** | An agreement | A legally enforceable agreement between two or more parties with consideration | A meeting of minds — requires full disclosure, mutual benefit, and voluntary participation | A commercial instrument; the foundation of all commercial jurisdiction; no contract = no jurisdiction |
| **Guardian** | Protector | A person appointed by a court to manage the affairs of another (typically a minor or incapacitated person); implies the ward cannot manage their own affairs | A protector — one who guards another by natural duty and love, not by state appointment | A trustee managing assets for a beneficiary; a commercial role with fiduciary obligations |
| **Parent** | Mother or father | A person who has legal responsibility for a child; includes biological, adoptive, and step-parents as defined by statute | Mother or father — the one who gave life; the bond is biological, spiritual, and beyond legal definition | The originator of a corporate entity; the party with primary claim to the "asset" (child as commercial property) |
| **Domicile** | Where you live | The place where a person has their true, fixed, permanent home; determines jurisdiction for legal purposes | Home — where a man or woman dwells by choice; not a legal construct but a natural fact | Registered address of a corporate entity; determines which commercial jurisdiction governs |
| **Residence** | Where you stay | A place where a person lives for a period of time; may differ from domicile; relevant to jurisdiction | The place where one currently sleeps — temporary, changeable, and irrelevant to natural rights | The operating address of the corporate entity; may be used to establish commercial jurisdiction |
| **Citizen** | Member of a country | A person owing allegiance to a state and entitled to its protection; a status that carries both rights AND obligations defined by statute | A free man or woman — not defined by allegiance to a state but by inherent sovereignty; "citizen" implies a contract with the state | A registered member of a corporate structure (the state as corporation); subject to the rules of that corporation |
| **Subject** | One who is governed | One who owes allegiance to a sovereign and is governed by that sovereign's laws | No man is subject to another absent consent — subjection implies surrender of sovereignty | A party to a commercial arrangement; subject to the terms of the contract |
| **Sovereign** | Supreme authority | The supreme power in a state; in democracies, sovereignty resides in the people collectively | Each man and woman is sovereign over themselves — self-governing, self-determining, answerable only to natural law and their creator | Not applicable — in commercial law, there are only parties to contracts, creditors, and debtors |

## Why This Matters

When a judge says "I am granting custody of the child to the respondent," that sentence operates in Layer 2 (statutory) or Layer 1 (commercial):

- "Granting" — implies the court has something to give, that it possesses the right and is transferring it.
- "Custody" — a statutory/commercial term for possession of a person/asset.
- "Child" — a legal category, not a natural relationship.
- "Respondent" — a party to a legal action, a role in the court's jurisdiction.

Translated to Layer 4 (natural law): "I, a man in a robe, am claiming authority to sever the bond between a mother and her offspring." The absurdity becomes clear when the jurisdictional layer shifts.

**The strategic move:** Never accept the court's vocabulary without questioning which jurisdiction it invokes. Every word is a jurisdictional choice. Choose your words to maintain your standing in the highest applicable jurisdiction.

## Usage Notes
